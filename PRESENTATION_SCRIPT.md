## Panel Defense Cheat Sheet: Anticipated Questions & How to Defend

Here are the most likely questions the panel will ask, matched to the presenter who should answer:

### Question 1 (For Speaker 2 - Architecture / AI):
> **Panel Question:** *"How can you guarantee that your AI chatbot will not hallucinate or give misleading legal advice that could get the City Government sued?"*
> 
> **Defensive Answer (Speaker 2):**
> *"Thank you for that critical question, sir/ma'am. We addressed this through strict architectural Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). The LLM is never permitted to answer from general parametric knowledge. Every query performs a keyword and relational search against our verified municipal database. If an ordinance is not in the database, the prompt explicitly instructs the model to state that no relevant ordinance was found. Furthermore, every response is accompanied by the exact ordinance title, number, penalty tiers, and an automated statutory disclaimer that the AI provides informational guidance rather than formal legal counsel."*

### Question 2 (For Speaker 3 - Hosting / Methodology):
> **Panel Question:** *"You used SQLite instead of PostgreSQL or MySQL. Is SQLite really capable of supporting an entire city of 89 barangays?"*
> 
> **Defensive Answer (Speaker 3):**
> *"Thank you, panel. We chose SQLite with Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) mode specifically based on our empirical access pattern analysis. Legislative ordinance systems are read-heavy—approximately 95% of traffic consists of citizens reading ordinances and querying the AI, with only 5% representing administrative writes. SQLite in WAL mode allows concurrent readers without blocking writers and performs in-process read operations up to twice as fast as networked PostgreSQL queries while requiring zero memory overhead and zero separate database hosting fees. For the write volume of 89 barangays submitting periodic drafts, SQLite WAL provides more than enough headroom, keeping our operational costs below ₱1,900 monthly."*

### Question 4 (For Speaker 1 - Governance / Rollout):
> **Panel Question:** *"Why are you proposing a pilot in only 2 to 3 barangays instead of rolling it out to all 89 barangays immediately?"*
> 
> **Defensive Answer (Speaker 1):**
> *"Thank you, panel. While the software architecture is fully capable of handling 89 barangays, municipal adoption involves organizational change management. In our methodology, we evaluated officials from 9 barangays. Deploying first to 2 to 3 representative barangays—for instance, one high-density urban barangay and two rural barangays—allows the LGU to establish administrative training standard operating procedures, observe real-world community incident volumes, and fine-tune staff workflows before incurring city-wide administrative overhead."*
